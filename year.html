Countries

Each row is in its own currency — kroner for Denmark, złoty for Poland — so the figures match the documents they come from. Switching a row's currency converts it at the rate under Settings. The totals underneath are in euro. Taxable income is the slice of income taxed in that country; Pre-paid tax is everything handed over that year, before any of it came back; Tax return is how much came back. Actual Tax — what it really cost — is editable too, but means something different depending on the country: Denmark genuinely withholds all year and settles up later, so typing its Actual Tax works Tax return out backwards from it, holding Pre-paid tax fixed. Every other country normally has no separate pre-paid phase — what's assessed is what's paid — so typing its Actual Tax there just sets Pre-paid tax to match, leaving Tax return alone (typically zero, still yours to edit directly if something really was refunded).

Analyze a tax assessment

Pick one or more assessment letters (the final-numbers document from a tax authority or accountant — not a payslip). Each is read by AI, then shown below for you to check and assign to a country row before anything is saved. Figures are read in whatever currency is printed on the document — verify them against that country's own currency below. Applying to Denmark's row works Tax return out backwards from Actual Tax; applying anywhere else sets Pre-paid tax to match Actual Tax instead, since only Denmark normally has a separate pre-paid phase.
